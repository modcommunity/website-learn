The website's [matchmaking](/learn/parties/matchmaking) finds a **server** for a
party. There is no player queue in it and no skill rating anywhere, so
`dot-matchmaking` is the whole player-queue half: who plays whom, on which
server, and how the result moves everybody's rating.

Requires `dot-core`. [dot-party](/learn/godot/addons/dot-party) is not a
dependency — a party is simply a ticket with several ids on it — and dot-auth's
backbone client is reached by duck typing.

```gdscript
var fives := DotMmPlaylist.of(&"ranked5", 2, 5)     # two sides of five
fives.max_party_size = 3                             # no full five-stacks against strangers

var mm := DotMatchmaker.new()
mm.playlists = [DotMmPlaylist.of(&"duel", 2, 1), fives]
mm.allocator = my_servers                            # anything with allocate(match)
add_child(mm)

mm.enqueue("t-81", &"ranked5", PackedStringArray(["ada", "bo"]),
        [{"eu": 31, "na": 110}, {"eu": 44}], party_id)
mm.match_found.connect(func(m, deadline): show_accept_prompt(m, deadline))
mm.match_ready.connect(func(m): send_everyone_to(m.allocation["address"]))
```

`DotMatchmaker` holds no socket, so it can run in a lobby server, a hub process
or anything else that can hear clients.

## Ratings: Glicko-2, per queue

One number says a newcomer and a thousand-game veteran on that number are the
same player. Glicko-2 keeps three — a **rating**, a **deviation** (how sure we
are) and a **volatility** — and the deviation shrinks with every game and grows
back with absence, so newcomers and returning players move fast and regulars
slowly.

- **The published algorithm, step for step**, checked against the worked example
  in Glickman's paper to four places.
- **A rating belongs to one queue.** Skill at two-a-side and at a twelve-player
  free-for-all are different facts.
- **A board shows the rating minus two deviations** (`DotMmRating.conservative()`),
  so two lucky wins do not top it.
- **Team games shift the opponent, not the player.** Each player is rated against
  `other_mean - (own_mean - player)`: everyone on a side sees the expectation the
  two means predict and moves by their own deviation. A 2000 on a side of 1000s
  loses about 6 points to an expected defeat, not the naive 20.
- **A leaver is rated as having lost**, fully. Otherwise leaving a losing match
  is free. A player there for under a quarter of the match is not rated at all.

## The queue widens with waiting

<Params>
  <Param name="Oldest ticket first">
    Each pass builds a match around whoever has waited longest — the only order
    that cannot starve anybody. Their skill window (`skill_window`,
    `skill_window_growth`, `skill_window_max`) and quality floor (`min_quality`,
    `min_quality_decay`) are the relaxed ones.
  </Param>
  <Param name="Each ticket's own latency">
    A candidate is judged against **its own** latency ceiling (`max_latency_ms`,
    growing to `max_latency_cap_ms`). A long wait makes the waiter accept a worse
    match; it does not hand a newcomer eighty extra milliseconds.
  </Param>
  <Param name="Parties are never split">
    A party is one ticket. Its latency to a region is its worst member's, and its
    matching rating leans from its mean towards its best player
    (`party_skill_blend`, `premade_bonus`), because a strong player carries.
  </Param>
  <Param name="Balanced sides">
    `DotMmBalance` tries every split for two sides up to sixteen tickets, and
    greedy-plus-swaps beyond, always moving whole parties. Deterministic.
  </Param>
  <Param name="The accept step">
    A decline or a timeout cools down only the person responsible
    (`decline_cooldown_sec`) and drops their ticket — the whole party, since
    requeueing the rest would split it. **Everybody else goes back in at the time
    they first queued.** A match with no free server falls through with nobody
    cooled down.
  </Param>
</Params>

## The website rates

Ratings decide who other people are made to play against, so on TMC **the
website computes them, not the game server**. `DotMmBackbone` sends the
[skill-rating routes](/learn/api/integration/ratings) the *result* — sides,
places, participation, leavers — and stores the ratings that come back:

```gdscript
var bb := DotMmBackbone.new()
bb.client = backbone     # dot-auth's DotBackboneClient: get_integration / post_integration
bb.store = mm.store
await bb.define(mm.playlists, mm.config)               # POST rating/define, once at boot
await bb.submit(&"ranked5", match_id, sides, placements)  # POST rating/submit, per match
```

The algorithm is duplicated in the site on purpose, as dot-stats' merge rule is,
and both halves test the same two figures. Without the routes — and permanently,
for a self-hosted server — the matchmaker rates locally into
`user://matchmaking/ratings.json`.

<Callout type="warning" title="Ratings come from the store, never from a client">
`enqueue` takes player ids, not ratings. A client that could send its own rating
could send any rating. For the same reason a player id sent to the site is a
per-scope pseudonym: an id shaped like `backbone:…` is refused before it leaves
the server.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="A result the site would refuse is lost, so it is refused here">
A body the site rejects comes back as a `400`, and a `400` is not retried — the
match is simply gone. So `DotMmPlaylist.validate()` applies the site's id rule
(1-64 of `A-Za-z0-9._:-`, starting alphanumeric) before a match is ever played in
that queue, and `submit` checks the site's bounds (2-64 sides, one placement per
side, no player twice, at most 128 players) and fails with the reason.
`define` sends queues 25 at a time and `refresh` reads 100 players at a time,
because one too many refuses the whole request.
</Callout>

<Callout type="warning" title="The site does not follow every setting yet">
The site hard-codes τ, the 14-day inactivity period, the 0.25 participation
floor and leaver-takes-loss; `rating/define` has no field for them. Change one in
`DotMatchmakingConfig` and a ranked queue is rated by one rule offline and
another online. `define(playlists, config)` warns when that is so.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Not here yet">
Backfill into a running match, short-handed matches after a long wait, role
queues, and an orchestrator that starts servers on demand. `allocator` is one
method; `DotMmAllocatorList` is the fixed server list a small deployment has.
</Callout>
