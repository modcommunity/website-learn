`dot-effects` holds what is currently true of an entity beyond its health:
burning, bleeding, crit-boosted, slowed, stunned, invulnerable, marked,
incapacitated.

Requires `dot-core`.

## One document, not twenty classes

Count the status effects a team shooter and a co-operative shooter ship between
them and you get about twenty-one. Nineteen are the same six fields with
different numbers: a duration, a periodic amount, a multiplier on damage taken,
a multiplier on damage dealt, a multiplier on movement, and a flag.

```gdscript
effects.define(DotEffectDef.burning(&"afterburn", 3.0, 10 * 64))
effects.define(DotEffectDef.damage_buff(&"crit", 3.0, 5 * 64))
effects.define(DotEffectDef.invulnerability(&"invuln", 8 * 64))
effects.define(DotEffectDef.speed(&"adrenaline", 1.4, 15 * 64))
```

## It never touches a health value

A burning entity does not lose health here. The manager reports an amount and
the game applies it, through whatever it already uses:

```gdscript
effects.damaged.connect(func(entity, amount, type, source):
    var hit := DotDamage.make(source, entity, amount, combat.damage_type(type))
    hit.tick = tick
    health_of(entity).apply(hit))
```

That is what lets a slow, a stun or a speed boost work in a game with no damage
system at all — and it means an afterburn tick goes through the same armour and invulnerability rules as every other hit applied to `DotHealth`.

## Down rather than dead

```gdscript
effects.rules.downed_enabled = true

# One place decides, rather than every damage site:
var what := effects.report_zero_health(entity)
if str(what.value) == "down":
    player.play_downed()
else:
    player.die()
```

A downed player bleeds out over ninety seconds, is picked up in five, comes back
with 30 health, and the third time is the last.

With `rules.downed_revive_scales` on (it is off by default), a second `begin_revive` joins the revive instead of replacing the reviver, and each reviver adds to the speed. `leave_revive(entity, by)` lets one go while the rest carry on; when the last one leaves, the revive is cancelled.

The bleed-out pool keeps draining while a revive is held, on purpose: a rescue started in the last seconds is a gamble. An effect definition can set `ignores_falloff`, and `DotEffectManager.ignores_falloff(entity)` tells a game whether the damage an entity deals ignores distance falloff (a crit does), the same way it asks for `may_jump` or the scales.

<Callout type="info" title="A cancelled revive throws its progress away">
If progress were kept, two players could chip at a revive a second at a time
from behind cover — and the cost of a revive is meant to be a stretch of time
where somebody is not shooting.
</Callout>
