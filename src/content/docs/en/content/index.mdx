Nearly everything you can publish here is a **content item**. There are eight
kinds, and they are built from the same parts on purpose: learn how one works
and you have learned most of how all of them work.

## The eight kinds

<Cards>
  <Card title="Mods" href="/learn/content/mods">
    A modification for one specific app. Carries install instructions, a
    licence, and a client/server environment.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Assets" href="/learn/content/assets">
    Game development material. Nearly identical to a mod, except the app is
    optional — an asset can be engine-agnostic.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Servers" href="/learn/content/servers">
    A live game server. The only item with a measured half: we query it, and
    record who is on it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Apps" href="/learn/content/apps">
    A game or program. Everything else files itself under one, and an app
    decides what its items may look like.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Communities" href="/learn/content/communities">
    A user-run organisation that owns servers, mods and articles, and states a
    minimum age for its spaces.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Collections" href="/learn/content/collections">
    A curated list of other items, installable as a set through the app.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Groups" href="/learn/content/groups">
    A roster of people — and, unusually, a permission principal that can be
    granted access to other items.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Articles" href="/learn/content/articles">
    Long-form writing, attachable to any of the above or standing alone.
  </Card>
</Cards>

## And two things that are deliberately not content items

**Parties** are play sessions. They are ephemeral, have no long-form body, take
no ratings or reviews, and carry their own small counters instead of writing
rows into the shared view / favourite / rating tables. A party that lasted
eleven minutes should not leave permanent rows in five shared tables. See
[Parties](/learn/parties).

**Categories** are taxonomy rather than published work — they organise items,
they are not items. See [Categories and tags](/learn/content/categories).

## Reading this section

Each item page is laid out the same way:

1. **What it is** — the one-paragraph answer.
2. **What only it does** — the fields and behaviour that are not shared.
3. **What can be attached to it** — its relations, with links to the page for
   each.
4. **Through the API** — how to create and edit it programmatically.
