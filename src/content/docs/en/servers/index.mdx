A server listing has two halves. The **editorial** half is yours — name,
description, rules, media, tags. The **measured** half is ours: a scanner
queries the address every few minutes and records what it finds.

Nearly everything surprising about servers comes from that split.

## The shape of it

1. A server is **discovered** — by a seed, by somebody adding it, or by a sweep
   of neighbouring ports.
2. It is **queried** on a cadence that depends on how interesting it is. A
   claimed, populated server is scanned every couple of minutes; an address that
   has never answered is scanned twice a day.
3. What comes back is written to the listing: online state, population, map,
   game mode, version, platform flags and the player list.
4. **Detectors** watch for listings that are lying — proxy farms, frozen player
   counts, filtered names — and flag them.
5. An owner can **claim** the listing, and from there switch individual measured
   fields over to themselves.

<Callout type="info" title="Being offline is not being removed">
A server that has been dark for a month keeps its page, its articles, its
comments and every inbound link. Removal from *listings* is a query-time
decision about how long it has been quiet — it never destroys the page.
</Callout>
